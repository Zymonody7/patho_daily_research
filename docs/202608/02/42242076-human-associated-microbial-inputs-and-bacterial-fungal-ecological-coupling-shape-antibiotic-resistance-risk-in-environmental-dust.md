## 摘要
环境粉尘是一种关键的暴露基质，但多界粉尘微生物组与抗生素耐药性（AMR）相关健康风险之间的关系尚未得到充分表征。我们对来自制药厂、奶牛场、火车站和学校的粉尘样本应用了鸟枪法宏基因组学，以全面表征细菌、真菌和病毒群落以及耐药组结构。在所采样的环境中，微生物群落组成在所有三个域中均表现出显著差异。具体而言，来自火车站的粉尘显示出最强的人类相关微生物信号，并拥有最高多样性的抗生素耐药基因（ARGs）以及基于 MetaCompare 计算的 AMR 风险。功能分析揭示了共享的细菌-真菌代谢组织，跨界分类学和功能关联指向结构化的生态耦合。变异分配分析显示，共享的解释分量解释了基于 MetaCompare 的人类健康 AMR 风险的大部分变异，特别是细菌组成、人类化程度和真菌功能结构之间的重叠。值得注意的是，念珠菌属（Candida）和出芽短梗霉属（Aureobasidium）作为分化的真菌指标出现，它们在追踪微生物组人类化和耐药组风险方面表现出相反的方向。相比之下，病毒辅助代谢基因仅占丰度加权病毒组的 3.92%，这与宿主相关的辅助层一致，而非主导的独立途径。总之，这些研究结果表明，环境粉尘中与 AMR 相关的特征是由人类相关微生物输入与生态耦合的细菌-真菌相互作用共同塑造的。

## Abstract
Environmental dust represents a critical exposure matrix, yet the relationships between multi-kingdom dust microbiomes and antimicrobial resistance (AMR)-associated health risks remain insufficiently characterized. We applied shotgun metagenomics to dust samples from pharmaceutical factories, a dairy farm, railway stations, and schools to comprehensively characterize bacterial, fungal, and viral communities, alongside resistome structure. Microbial community composition exhibited significant differences across all three domains among the sampled environments. Specifically, dust from railway stations displayed the strongest human-associated microbial signal and harbored the highest diversity of antibiotic resistance genes (ARGs), and MetaCompare-derived AMR risk. Functional analyses revealed shared bacterial-fungal metabolic organization, with cross-domain taxonomic and functional associations pointing to structured ecological coupling. Variation partitioning analysis showed that shared explanatory components accounted for most of the variation in MetaCompare-based human-health AMR risk, particularly the overlap among bacterial composition, humanization, and fungal functional structure. Notably, Candida and Aureobasidium emerged as divergent fungal indicators, tracking microbiome humanization and resistome risk in opposite directions. By contrast, viral auxiliary metabolic genes accounted for only 3.92% of the abundance-weighted virome, consistent with a host-linked auxiliary layer rather than a dominant independent pathway. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that AMR-related signatures in environmental dust are shaped by the interplay of human-associated microbial inputs and ecologically coupled bacterial-fungal interactions.
