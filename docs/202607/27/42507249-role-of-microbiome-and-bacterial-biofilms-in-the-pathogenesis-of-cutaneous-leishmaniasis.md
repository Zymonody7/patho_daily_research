## 摘要
目的：皮肤利什曼病（CL）是一种以慢性皮肤损伤为特征的媒介传播寄生虫病。新兴证据强调了皮肤微生物组和细菌生物膜在疾病进展中的关键作用。本综述讨论了支持皮肤微生物组和细菌生物膜参与 CL 发病机制的现有证据。它进一步探讨了微生物群失调和细菌共感染的临床意义，回顾了新兴的针对生物膜的辅助治疗策略，并概述了未来转化研究的关键知识空白。方法：对有关皮肤利什曼病中皮肤微生物组、细菌生物膜、微生物群失调、细菌共感染、宿主免疫反应以及新兴的针对生物膜的辅助治疗策略的现有文献进行了回顾。结果：CL 皮损与微生物失调相关，其特征是包括葡萄球菌属（Staphylococcus）和链球菌属（Streptococcus）在内的机会性细菌丰度增加。这些改变导致宿主免疫反应失调，包括促炎细胞因子产生增加和中性粒细胞招募增强，从而促进疾病进展并延迟伤口愈合。CL 皮损内的生物膜形成是导致疾病持续存在的另一个因素。生物膜保护细菌免受宿主免疫防御并限制抗菌药物渗透，从而降低治疗效果并导致治疗失败。利什曼原虫、细菌群落与宿主免疫之间的相互作用创造了一个复杂的多种微生物微环境，加剧了皮损病理。结论：深入了解这些复杂的相互作用可能有助于设计针对寄生虫和多种微生物伤口环境的更有效的治疗方法。

## Abstract
PURPOSE: Cutaneous leishmaniasis (CL) is a vector-borne parasitic disease characterized by chronic skin lesions. Emerging evidence has highlighted the critical role of the skin microbiome and bacterial biofilms in disease progression. This review discusses the current evidence supporting the involvement of the skin microbiome and bacterial biofilms in the pathogenesis of CL. It further examines the clinical significance of microbiota dysbiosis and bacterial coinfections, reviews emerging biofilm-directed adjunctive therapeutic strategies, and outlines key knowledge gaps for future translational research. METHODS: The available literature on the skin microbiome, bacterial biofilms, microbiota dysbiosis, bacterial coinfections, host immune responses, and emerging biofilm-directed adjunctive therapeutic strategies in cutaneous leishmaniasis was reviewed. RESULTS: CL lesions are associated with microbial dysbiosis characterized by an increased abundance of opportunistic bacteria including Staphylococcus and Streptococcus species. These alterations contribute to dysregulated host immune responses, including increased production of pro-inflammatory cytokines and enhanced neutrophil recruitment, thereby promoting disease progression and delaying wound healing. Biofilm formation within CL lesions represents an additional factor contributing to disease persistence. Biofilms protect bacteria from host immune defences and limit antimicrobial penetration, thus reducing therapeutic efficacy and contributing to treatment failure. The interplay among Leishmania, bacterial communities, and host immunity creates a complex polymicrobial microenvironment that exacerbates lesion pathology. CONCLUSION: A deeper understanding of these complex interactions may enable the design of more effective therapeutic approaches directed at both the parasite and the polymicrobial wound environment.
